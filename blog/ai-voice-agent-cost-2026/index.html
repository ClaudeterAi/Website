How Much Does an AI Voice Agent Cost in 2026?

Claudeter TeamSeptember 23, 20266 min read

In short

  • Production voice agents on developer platforms land around $0.05 to $0.25 per minute all-in; the advertised platform fee is usually the smallest layer.
  • Every call runs through five metered layers: telephony, speech-to-text, the language model, text-to-speech, and orchestration.
  • A custom agent has two prices: a one-time build and a recurring run cost. Compare vendors on cost per connected minute, not headline rate.
  • Ask every vendor the same question: total cost for 2,000 minutes a month with model, voice, transcription and telephony included.

The honest answer is that an AI voice agent has two prices, and vendors tend to show you whichever one looks better. There is a one-time cost to design, integrate and test the agent, and a recurring cost for every minute it spends on the phone. Buyers who compare only the per-minute rate on a pricing page usually end up paying 2 to 3 times what they budgeted, not because anyone lied, but because the rate covered one of five layers.

What a per-minute rate actually includes

Every AI phone call runs through a stack of metered services. Pricing guides published across the industry in 2026 converge on roughly the same ranges for each layer:

  • Telephony: the carrier minutes themselves, typically $0.008 to $0.014 per minute on programmable voice providers.
  • Speech-to-text: real-time transcription of the caller, roughly $0.004 to $0.024 per minute depending on the model.
  • Language model: the reasoning layer, billed per token, which works out to about $0.003 to $0.08 per conversation minute depending on the model tier and how much context each turn carries.
  • Text-to-speech: the voice, often the most expensive line, around $0.02 to $0.10 per minute for premium natural voices.
  • Orchestration: the platform that stitches the above together and handles turn-taking, interruptions and tool calls, roughly $0.05 to $0.14 per minute on bundled platforms.

Add those up and a production agent on a developer-grade platform costs somewhere between $0.05 and $0.25 per minute all-in. The low end assumes a frugal stack: cheap transcription, a mid-tier model, a standard voice. The high end buys a frontier model and a premium voice. Enterprise contact-center suites that bundle seats, compliance modules and analytics run several times higher on a fully loaded basis.

The cheapest advertised rate is almost always a bring-your-own-keys platform. The $0.05 covers orchestration only; you pay the model, voice, transcription and carrier separately. It can still be the right choice, but only if you price the whole stack.

The fees that never make the pricing page

Four line items show up on invoices far more often than on websites:

  • Silence billing. Most platforms meter the full call, including hold time, ringing and the seconds after the caller hangs up. A 3-minute conversation is often a 3.5-minute bill.
  • Concurrency limits. Base plans cap simultaneous calls. If your peak hour needs 40 concurrent lines, you are buying a higher tier or paying per additional channel.
  • Compliance surcharges. HIPAA-eligible configurations, signed BAAs, in-region data residency and extended recording retention are commonly priced as add-ons. Budget 15 to 30 percent on top for regulated deployments.
  • Integration and maintenance. The agent is only useful if it can read your calendar, CRM or practice-management system and write back to it. Someone has to build and maintain those connections when the vendor changes an API.

Build cost: what you pay before the first call

If you buy a template agent from a self-serve platform, the build cost is your own time: typically 40 to 80 hours of prompt design, testing and iteration for a technically capable team. If you hire a studio to build a custom agent, published rate cards in 2026 put a scoped proof of concept in the low five figures and a production agent with several integrations, monitoring and failover anywhere from the mid five figures to well past $100,000 for a large multi-region deployment.

Where you land inside that range depends on four variables:

  • Number of call types. An agent that books, reschedules and answers ten FAQs is a fraction of the work of one that also verifies insurance, takes payments and handles complaints.
  • Number of systems it touches. Each integration (EHR, CRM, payment gateway, ticketing) is its own mini-project with its own failure modes.
  • Accuracy bar. A restaurant reservation line can tolerate an occasional retry. A patient-facing line cannot.
  • Languages and dialects. Bilingual and code-switching callers roughly double the test matrix.

Our own approach at Claudeter is to compress this into a fixed shape: a 3-day discovery sprint that produces a working proof of concept on your real call types, then a 6-week build to production. The point of the sprint is that you see the agent handle your calls before you commit to the build budget.

Run cost: the number that matters over a year

Build cost is paid once. Run cost is paid forever, and it is the number vendors are least transparent about. The metric to standardize on is cost per connected minute: everything in the stack, divided by minutes where a human was actually talking to the agent.

A worked example. A clinic front desk handles 1,500 inbound calls a month averaging 3.5 minutes. That is 5,250 conversation minutes. At $0.12 per connected minute all-in, the run cost is about $630 a month. At $0.25, it is roughly $1,300. Compare that to the fully loaded cost of the receptionist hours those calls consume, and to the revenue attached to the calls that currently go to voicemail, and the ROI conversation becomes concrete rather than speculative.

Two levers move run cost more than any negotiation:

  • Shorter calls. Good conversation design gets the caller to the outcome faster. An agent that confirms details once instead of three times saves 20 percent of minutes.
  • Right-sizing the model. Most turns in a scheduling call do not need a frontier model. Routing simple turns to a smaller model and escalating hard ones is standard practice and can halve the language-model line.

How to compare vendors fairly

Send every vendor the same scenario and insist on a single all-in number:

"What is my total monthly cost for 2,000 connected minutes, with the language model, voice, transcription, telephony, recording storage and any compliance add-ons included? Show the per-minute figure and the fixed fees separately."

Then ask three follow-ups: what is billed when the caller is on hold, what happens at 30 concurrent calls, and who owns the prompts, workflows and recordings if you leave. A vendor who answers all four cleanly is worth shortlisting regardless of where their headline rate sits.

When a custom build beats a platform

Off-the-shelf agents are the right answer for a single, simple call type with no system integration. A custom build earns its price when any of these are true: the agent must read and write to your systems of record, you operate in a regulated industry, you need bilingual or dialect-heavy conversations, or the call volume is high enough that a 5-cent difference in run cost pays the build in a quarter.

Frequently asked questions

What is the average cost of an AI voice agent per minute?

On developer-grade platforms, production deployments typically cost $0.05 to $0.25 per minute once telephony, speech-to-text, the language model, text-to-speech and the platform fee are all included. Advertised rates as low as $0.05 usually cover the platform layer only.

How much does it cost to build a custom AI voice agent?

Published agency rate cards in 2026 place a scoped proof of concept in the low five figures and a production agent with multiple integrations from the mid five figures upward. The main cost drivers are the number of call types, the number of systems the agent connects to, the accuracy bar and the languages supported.

Are AI voice agents cheaper than a call center?

Per connected minute, an AI agent is usually a fraction of the fully loaded cost of a human agent, and it answers on the first ring at any hour. The fair comparison is total cost of ownership, including build, integration maintenance and human escalation for the calls the agent should not handle.

What hidden fees should I watch for in AI voice agent pricing?

Silence and hold-time billing, concurrency caps that force plan upgrades, compliance add-ons such as HIPAA configurations and extended recording retention, and the ongoing cost of maintaining integrations when third-party APIs change.
